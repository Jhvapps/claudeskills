# Crowdfunding Platforms & Mechanics (2026)

Compiled from live research, August 2026. Fee figures and platform ownership change — re-verify anything with a £ sign directly with the platform before quoting to an investor deck.

## ⚠️ Correction to a common assumption

**Crowdcube and Seedrs never merged.** They agreed to merge in Oct 2020, but the deal was blocked by the CMA on competition grounds and terminated March 2021. Instead, **US platform Republic acquired Seedrs** (announced Dec 2021), and on **10 July 2024 Seedrs rebranded as "Republic Europe."** The Seedrs Ltd/FCA-authorised entity still sits underneath, but the consumer-facing name is now Republic Europe. Crowdcube remains fully independent.

So the UK equity crowdfunding market in 2026 is: **Crowdcube** (independent) vs **Republic Europe** (US-owned, formerly Seedrs).

## Equity crowdfunding platforms

### Crowdcube (UK, independent)
- **Fee**: 7% success fee (excl. VAT) + completion fee averaging 0.75–1.5% → all-in roughly **7.75–8.5%** of the raise. Nothing charged unless you succeed.
- **Optional**: marketing/video production packages, £2.5k–£7k flat.
- **Investor fee**: 2.49% investment fee (some sources cite 5% on certain deals), capped £250; 5% fee on profit at exit.
- **Campaign length**: typically 30–60 days.
- **Investor base**: 2m+ registered investors, 1,500+ campaigns, £2.2bn+ raised historically.
- **SEIS/EIS**: supports EIS; SEIS support is **unconfirmed/limited across sources** — verify per-raise directly with Crowdcube.
- **Best for**: largest UK reach, no cost to list, strong track record with consumer/hospitality brands (BrewDog, Camden Town, Honest Burgers, Grind all raised here).

### Republic Europe (formerly Seedrs, owned by Republic/US)
- **Fee**: sources disagree — one figure is £5k pre-registration + £5k launch + £2k/yr nominee fee + 6% of funds raised + 0.5% processing; another cites a flat ~7.5% success fee. **Get a direct quote before comparing to Crowdcube** — the fee structure appears to have shifted post-rebrand and the fixed-fee load (up to £12k before any % applies) materially changes the economics of a smaller raise.
- **Investor fee**: 2.5% (min £5, capped £250); 5% on profit at exit.
- **Minimum investment**: £10.
- **Campaign length**: 30 days standard (rule in force since July 2021).
- **SEIS/EIS**: strong support — Seedrs' nominee structure was originally built around it.
- **Best for**: nominee structure simplifies cap table admin; Republic's US network is useful for cross-border/later-stage rounds.

### Crowdfunder.co.uk
- Primarily reward/donation-based, but also hosts equity raises **and a dedicated community shares vertical** (relevant for pub buyouts — see legal doc).
- Fee: 5% of funds raised + 2.28% + £0.24 processing per pledge. 0% for charities.

### Wefunder (US)
- 7.9% fundraiser fee on success (0% if you bring your own lead investor). Investor fee 2% ACH / 5.5%+$2 card.
- Reg CF cap: **$5m/12 months**. ~1.5m registered investors.
- US-only regulatory framework — not directly usable for a UK company without a US entity.
- Best for: "community round" positioning — DTC/consumer brands turning customers into investors.

### StartEngine / Republic (US)
- StartEngine: 5–8% cash + ~2% in securities, ~$10k deferred completion fee (figures vary by source — SEC Exhibit C filing is the authoritative source). Reg CF $5m/12mo, Reg A+ up to $75m/12mo.
- Republic (US parent of Republic Europe): 7% cash + 2% securities on Reg CF raises.

### Regulatory context (2026)
- **New FCA "Public Offer Platform" (POP) regime, live 19 Jan 2026**: offers of securities to the public **below £5m are exempt** from a prospectus; **£5m+** must go through an authorised POP. Most hospitality/marketplace-scale raises are unaffected — this only bites at £5m+.
- Retail investors must pass an **FCA appropriateness test** before investing in unlisted equity crowdfunding (classed as a Restricted Mass Market Investment) — this is real conversion friction to plan for, not a UX bug.
- SEIS/EIS both remain live in 2026 — see the legal/tax reference doc for current limits.

## Reward-based crowdfunding

### Kickstarter
- All-or-nothing only. 5% platform fee + ~3%+$0.20 processing → **~8–10% effective cost**.
- Success rate ~38–43% overall, varies hugely by category.
- Best for a **single, photogenic physical product launch** with an existing audience — not a fit for an ongoing brand or subscription.

### Indiegogo
- **As of October 2025, Indiegogo moved to all-or-nothing only** — flexible funding has been discontinued platform-wide. This removes its historic differentiator vs Kickstarter; double-check this is still current before planning around it.
- 5% platform fee + ~3%+$0.20 processing (~8% effective).
- Success rate estimated 18–30% (no official figures published) — lower than Kickstarter because it accepts more concept-stage/first-time campaigns.

## Debt / revenue-based crowdfunding (non-dilutive alternatives)

### Assetz Capital — ⚠️ status change
**No longer a retail crowdfunding platform.** Exited retail P2P lending entirely in December 2022, now 100% institutional funding. Still a live commercial lender (property finance from 8.35% p.a.) but not "crowd capital" anymore — a common outdated assumption worth correcting.

### Folk2Folk
- Genuine P2P: retail/HNW investors lend directly to vetted local businesses, secured against property/land.
- £100,000–£10,000,000 loans, 6 months–5 years, fixed **7.5–9.0% p.a.**
- **Explicitly names hospitality, tourism, agriculture, construction and renovation as core sectors.** £589.5m+ facilitated since 2013.
- The most directly relevant debt option for a pub/hotel refurb or expansion — no dilution, but requires property/land security.

### Revenue-based finance (RBF)
- Not "crowdfunding" (institutional capital, not a public crowd) but the most relevant non-dilutive alternative for a cash-generative DTC brand or marketplace.
- **Wayflyer**: $5k–$20m, 1.5–3x monthly revenue advance, 5–10% fixed fee. E-commerce/DTC focused.
- **Outfund** (UK): £10k–£2m, revenue-based, no equity given up.
- Repayment caps across the wider market range roughly **1.1x–3x** the amount advanced — no single market standard, negotiate case by case.
- Good fit for proven, cash-generative businesses with predictable recurring revenue; poor fit for pre-revenue/early-stage (patient capital is what's needed there, not fixed repayment obligations).

## Community shares / Community Benefit Society (CBS) — the pub-specific route

Structurally different from equity crowdfunding — this is the standard route for **community pub buyouts**, not growth capital for an already-owned, profitable venue.

- Investors buy **withdrawable, non-transferable share capital**, not tradeable equity — no capital growth expected, this is patient social-return capital.
- Legal form: **Community Benefit Society (BenCom)**, usually with an **asset lock** so the pub can never be sold/demutualised for private gain. Registered with the **FCA**, not Companies House.
- **One member, one vote**, regardless of stake size.
- Statutory individual investment cap: **£100,000** per person.
- **Plunkett Foundation**'s "More than a Pub" programme: community pub projects have raised **£14m+** in community shares collectively; **180,000+ people** have invested in Plunkett-supported community businesses overall, raising £50m+.
- **Typical single pub raise: £150k–£450k**, from **100–400 local investors**, minimum stakes **£50–£250**, campaign runs **6 weeks to several months** (much longer than a 30-day Crowdcube raise — driven by community mobilisation, not platform algorithm).
- Support available: **Community Shares Booster Fund** (matches investment pound-for-pound up to £100k), **Reach Fund** grants (£5k–£15k for legal/offer-document setup).
- **Community Investment Tax Relief (CITR)** is a *different, narrower* relief than SEIS/EIS — it applies to investment into accredited **CDFIs**, not directly to a typical pub BenCom. Don't assume CITR applies without confirming CDFI accreditation status.

## Comparison table — which model fits which business

| Business type | Best-fit model | Why | Shortlist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-stage marketplace/tech startup | **Equity crowdfunding** (SEIS/EIS if eligible) | Needs patient, non-repayable growth capital; a public raise doubles as marketing/liquidity for a two-sided marketplace | Crowdcube first (reach, no listing fee), Republic Europe second (nominee simplicity, US tie-in) |
| Established, cash-generative hospitality venue | **Debt / revenue-based finance** | Proven trading + hard assets support debt pricing; avoids dilution on an already-profitable asset | Folk2Folk (property-secured, hospitality-named sector) |
| DTC consumer brand | **Reward crowdfunding** for a specific launch; **equity/RBF** once trading | Reward crowdfunding validates demand cheaply with no dilution; RBF funds inventory once there's recurring revenue | Kickstarter for a flagship launch; Wefunder/Crowdcube for a "community round"; Outfund/Wayflyer for working capital |
| Community-owned pub (save-the-local scenario) | **Community shares (CBS/BenCom)** | Only structure with the asset lock; investors accept modest/no return for a social outcome | Plunkett Foundation + Crowdfunder.co.uk community shares vertical |
