# Crowdfunding Marketing & Campaign Playbook

Compiled from live research, August 2026. Covers both reward-based (Kickstarter/Indiegogo) and equity (Crowdcube/Republic Europe) campaigns — tactics diverge sharply in places, flagged below.

## The one finding that matters most

**The campaign is won or lost before the launch button is pressed.** Every other tactic below (video, PR, paid ads, referral mechanics) is in service of either building your pre-launch list or converting it on launch day.

## 1. Pre-launch phase

- **Soft-commitment benchmark**: **20–25% of the funding goal soft-committed before going live** (reward-based). Below this, campaigns rarely reach target — it's a signal the target is set too high for your network.
- **Equity crowdfunding**: aim for a **lead investor covering ~50% of the round** before public launch (angel syndicate, VC, accelerator) — a harder, more formal version of the same principle.
- **Kickstarter stat**: campaigns hitting **20% of goal within the first 48 hours have a 78% chance of reaching target**.
- **Timeline**: reward campaigns typically run a 6-week to multi-month pre-launch list-building phase; equity crowdfunding (Crowdcube) expects **3+ months from decision-to-raise to going live**, driven by due diligence and compliance, not just marketing readiness.
- **Private/pre-registration stage**: both Crowdcube and Republic Europe let you go live privately first (via a unique URL, or up to 14 days pre-public on Republic Europe) so your warmest people invest before the public sees it — this sets the "% funded" number every subsequent visitor sees.
- **LaunchBoom's reservation funnel** (named practitioner tactic, reward-based): ask visitors to reserve their spot with a small deposit (e.g. £1) for a launch-day discount, instead of a plain email opt-in. Converts at **35–40%** vs **1–3% for a plain email opt-in** — claimed 30-50x uplift. LaunchBoom has generated $175m+ in campaign revenue using this model.
- **Activate existing customers/members first** — for a hospitality/F&B raise this means email list, loyalty scheme, till-receipt QR codes, in-venue signage, staff asking regulars directly. This is consistently the highest-converting audience and should be worked in the private stage, not held back.

### Reverse-engineering your target
- **The "1-3 Rule"** (Adrià Tarrida): 1–3% of campaign-page visitors convert to backers/investors in successful campaigns. Below 1% = targeting/proposition problem; sustained above 3% = you're still only reaching friends/family, not proven on cold traffic.
- Rough sizing: at 2% conversion and a £40 average pledge, you need ~1,250 visits per £1,000 raised — multiply your target by ~1.25 to estimate the traffic you need to plan for.
- Traffic quality varies hugely: organic ≥6% conversion is good; paid social 1–2%; people who've explicitly followed a project pre-launch convert around **30%** — the strongest argument for prioritising list-building over cold traffic.

## 2. Launch mechanics and the funding curve

- First and last 24-48 hours often account for **more than 50% of total funds raised combined** — the classic U-shaped curve (fast start, quiet middle, rush at the close).
- Kickstarter's own guidance: aim for roughly **a third of target within 48 hours**.
- **Important nuance**: equity crowdfunding (first-come-first-served allocation, e.g. Crowdcube/Republic Europe) tends toward an **L-shaped curve** — a strong spike at launch that tails off — rather than reward-based crowdfunding's U-shape, which relies on scarcity and last-chance psychology at the close. Plan equity launches to front-load urgency (lead investor + private-stage momentum); plan reward launches to engineer deliberate spikes at both ends.

### Engineering a strong first-day push
1. Convert your private/soft-committed list to public pledges the second you go live — sets the social-proof number everyone else sees.
2. Activate existing customers first.
3. Personal 1:1 asks convert far better than a generic broadcast post for the first wave.
4. Coordinate a press embargo to land exactly at launch, concentrated rather than trickling.
5. Reward-based: limited/tiered early-bird pricing forces immediate action instead of "I'll back it later."

### The mid-campaign slump — plan for it, don't react to it
High-performing campaigns pre-plan a drip of momentum events: scheduled stretch-goal reveals, a fresh update/video, sharing press mentions, new perk unlocks, milestone call-outs ("50% funded"). Indiegogo's own data: successful campaigns post **at least 4 updates** and add **~12 new perks** post-launch.

## 3. Video pitch

- **Length**: 2–4 minutes, ~3 minutes is the sweet spot most cited. People really only engage in the first 30 seconds — the hook has to land immediately, no logo animation or slow build.
- **Structure**: hook + problem (first 5-10s) → founder-to-camera personal story → product/service demonstration (show, don't just tell) → traction/proof points repeated for stickiness → clear specific ask (amount + exactly what it funds) → social proof close (existing investors/customers on camera, for equity specifically).
- **Reward backers vs equity investors respond to different triggers**:
  - Reward backers: driven by autonomy/community/belonging — lean into narrative and "join the movement." They respond **negatively** to heavy patent/IP talk (reads as "not for people like me").
  - Equity investors: weigh financial return, trust signals (competence + relationship trust), and herding from other investors already in. Lean into numbers, the lead-investor signal, evidence of expert backing.
- Post supplementary short-form videos through the campaign (even phone-shot testimonials), not just one hero video at launch — keeps content fresh through the mid-campaign slump.

## 4. PR and media strategy

- Brief embargoed journalists **5-10 working days ahead**, one tier-one outlet as a genuine exclusive, wider embargo lift at public launch. Best send window: Tuesday–Thursday, 9-11am local time. Overall PR prep lead time: 2-4 weeks, run in parallel with list-building.
- **Concentrate coverage at the moment of launch** rather than letting it trickle — simultaneous publication creates the appearance of sudden momentum, feeding both investor herding and the platform's trending algorithm.
- What lands: a clear market problem + strong traction + a "why now" angle — funding amount alone isn't newsworthy. **Personalise every pitch** — blanket press-release blasts are explicitly called out as ineffective.
- Use milestones as fresh news hooks beyond launch day: private-stage opening, public transition, overfunding, early close.
- **UK hospitality trade press relevant to Coal & Co / Need Coffee-style raises**: The Morning Advertiser (consistent history of covering pub/brewery crowdfunding raises), BigHospitality, Propel Info (industry briefing, widely read by operators/investors). General trade: Startups.co.uk, City A.M., Seedtable.
- **PR should be a complementary channel, not the core strategy** — it converts poorly relative to a warm list; a common mistake is over-indexing on press and under-investing in list-building.

## 5. Paid advertising — UK regulatory context matters here

This is the sharpest divergence between reward and equity crowdfunding, and it's UK-specific:

- **Equity investment ads are a "financial promotion" under FCA rules** — must be fair, clear, not misleading, and typically need approval from an FCA-authorised person.
- **FCA banned monetary/non-monetary incentives to invest** (in force by Feb 2023, incentive-specific instrument from Apr 2024) — this explicitly includes "refer a friend" cash bonuses and cashback for investing in Restricted Mass Market Investments. **Narrow exemption**: an issuer's *own* "real economy" product/service discount (e.g. a pub group offering investors a bar discount) is still permitted — this is exactly the BrewDog playbook and the legally clean way to build perks around a hospitality equity raise.
- **First-time investors face a mandatory 24-hour cooling-off period and personalised risk warnings** before completing their first investment — build this friction into conversion expectations; it's a deliberate regulatory feature, not a UX bug.
- **Meta has an active, unresolved UK compliance problem** with financial ads (regulator reviews found 1,000+ non-compliant financial promotions in a single week in 2026) — an unapproved equity crowdfunding ad is a real compliance risk even before Meta's own moderation catches it.
- **Reward-based crowdfunding is NOT subject to any of the above** — it's a pre-sale, not a financial promotion, so Meta/Google ads run under normal e-commerce rules. This makes paid social a much cleaner channel for reward campaigns than equity ones.
- Google Search ads on intent keywords work well for equity raises (higher CPC but higher-intent traffic); LinkedIn is the channel for reaching sophisticated/accredited investors; retargeting is essential — an estimated ~98% of visitors don't convert on their first visit.
- **Budget rule of thumb**: ~10% of the funding goal across the full campaign lifecycle. Track cost-per-investor/backer, not top-of-funnel CPC — equity conversion has extra regulatory friction baked in.

## 6. Community and momentum tactics

- **UK-specific constraint**: classic cash referral incentives ("refer a friend, get £X") are **banned for equity crowdfunding**. What remains viable: equip your network with shareable assets (pre-written copy, explainer video, one-pagers) with no incentive attached; and product/service discounts as investor perks (not referral bonuses) under the "real economy" exemption. **Reward-based crowdfunding has no such restriction** — referral bonuses and affiliate tracking are standard on Kickstarter/Indiegogo.
- Regular, substantive investor updates during the campaign are a core retention mechanic, not courtesy comms — research into overfunding found the number of founder updates posted during a live campaign is a measurable predictor of overfunding.
- Milestone announcements ("50% funded," "overfunded") are their own PR/social moments, not just internal markers.
- **Post-campaign investor relations sets up your next raise**: successive rounds function as a quality/trust signal to the market — returning investors signal validation to new ones. Conversely, a fragmented cap table (hundreds of small crowd shareholders) can make future institutional fundraising harder if VCs view it skeptically. Budget for ongoing investor communications as a permanent line item, not a campaign-only activity.

## 7. Benchmarks and failure modes

| Metric | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Soft-commitment before launch | 20-25% (reward) / ~50% via lead investor (equity) |
| Goal % in first 48h for 78% success odds (Kickstarter) | 20% |
| First+last 24-48h share of total raised | >50% combined |
| Visitor → backer/investor conversion (healthy) | 1-3% |
| Organic traffic conversion (good) | ≥6% |
| Paid social conversion | 1-2% |
| Pre-launch "follower" conversion | ~30% |
| Reservation-funnel landing page conversion | 35-40% |
| Marketing budget as % of raise target | ~10% |
| Pitch video length | 2-4 min (~3 optimal) |
| Updates posted during successful campaigns | ≥4 |
| Kickstarter overall success rate | ~38-42% |
| Crowdcube-funded businesses still trading | 89% (self-reported) |
| Broader UK equity crowdfunding failure rate (2013-15 cohort) | 21% collapsed |

### Named reasons campaigns fail
1. Weak/absent pre-launch list → miss the 20-25% threshold → miss the critical first-48-hour momentum window → lose platform-algorithm visibility.
2. Unclear ask — can't answer "why this, why now" in one sentence.
3. No mid-campaign content plan — the slump is inevitable and must be pre-planned, not reacted to.
4. Bad video, or none — poor production, no hook in the first seconds, no founder-to-camera moment.
5. Low traffic generally — most failures are a **distribution problem, not a product problem**.
6. Unrealistic goal relative to network size — reverse-engineer the goal from your real pre-launch soft-commitment total, don't set it aspirationally and market backward.
7. Equity specifically: no lead investor/anchor commitment before public launch — without that ~50% anchor, cold investor traffic converts far worse (herding effect cuts both ways).
