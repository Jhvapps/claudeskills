# Crowdfunding

Knowledge base for evaluating and running a crowdfunding raise — built specifically around Jack's business mix (multi-venue hospitality group, a DTC/B2B coffee brand, a burger brand, and a marketplace startup with an active fundraise). UK-first, since every relevant business is UK-based.

## Before advising — ask or establish

1. **Which business, and what's it for?** (growth capital / venue refurb / product launch / community save-the-local)
2. **How much, and how urgent?** (crowdfunding realistically takes 3+ months from decision to funds in bank for equity; days-weeks for debt/RBF)
3. **Dilution tolerance** — equity crowdfunding means potentially hundreds of small shareholders permanently on the cap table. Debt/RBF/community shares avoid this.
4. **Does the entity even qualify for SEIS/EIS?** This is the single highest-leverage question and the one most likely to be assumed wrongly — see the hospitality exclusion note below before promising any investor a tax break.

## The one-page decision framework

| If the business is... | Lean toward... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-revenue or early-stage, two-sided (Chef Pantry) | **Equity crowdfunding** (Crowdcube first choice) | Patient capital + the raise itself is a customer-acquisition channel for both sides of the marketplace — see JustPark/Freetrade |
| An established, profitable, asset-heavy venue (a Coal & Co pub/hotel needing refurb capital) | **Debt** (Folk2Folk — explicitly hospitality-sector, property-secured, ~7.5-9%) | Proven trading supports debt pricing; avoids diluting an already-profitable asset; much faster to close |
| A specific venue under threat / being bought from an absentee owner, with real community identity | **Community shares (CBS/BenCom)** via Plunkett Foundation | Only structure with the asset lock; investors expect a social outcome, not ROI — wrong tool for a venue Coal & Co already owns and profits from |
| A DTC brand launching a specific new product (Need Coffee) | **Reward crowdfunding** (Kickstarter) | Validates demand fast, no dilution, no equity given up |
| A DTC/B2B brand with real recurring revenue needing working capital (Need Coffee, once trading) | **Revenue-based finance** (Wayflyer/Outfund) or a **Crowdcube/Wefunder "community round"** | RBF = no dilution, fast; equity community round = turns loyal customers into owners/evangelists (see Grind) |
| A consumer F&B brand ready to fund regional expansion (Chef Burger) | **Equity crowdfunding** | Honest Burgers is the near-exact template: £2.9m vs £1m target, 3,455 investors, funded regional rollout |

## Applying this to Jack's actual businesses

**Chef Pantry** (marketplace, active fundraise) — equity crowdfunding is the clear fit. JustPark (£17m cumulative, 10,000+ investors, funded via Crowdcube) and Freetrade (13,000 crowd investors became its largest shareholder bloc) are the template: the raise itself becomes a chef+venue acquisition channel, not just capital. Get SEIS/EIS Advance Assurance from HMRC before marketing — a young, small marketplace company is a strong candidate. Avoid the **Emoov trap**: never cut the valuation mid-campaign under time pressure — that single move triggered Emoov's collapse and dragged down unrelated raises on the same platform by association.

**Coal & Co venues** — treat per-venue, not as one group raise:
- **The Castle Inn (hotel + pub)**: likely **excluded from SEIS/EIS** — HMRC excludes hotels where the company owns/occupies the premises, and accommodation isn't a small enough share of trade to count as ancillary. Don't promise investors tax relief here without HMRC Advance Assurance in hand. Folk2Folk-style secured debt (hospitality-named sector, property-backed) is the more realistic funding route for this venue.
- **Tollgate**: ambiguous — depends on how the wellness suite + 30 rooms get marketed and what % of revenue accommodation represents. Same Advance Assurance caveat applies before any raise references SEIS/EIS.
- **Pure F&B venues** (The Bull on the Green, Ash & Honey, St Mary's Tea Room, the farm shop, Taste of Dreams bakery): more plausible SEIS/EIS candidates if structured as separate qualifying trading entities, since food/drink retail with no accommodation isn't an excluded activity.
- If any single venue is ever under genuine threat of closure or being bought from an outside owner, community shares (not equity crowdfunding) is the right tool — but it's the wrong tool for an already Coal & Co-owned, trading venue.

**Need Coffee** — Grind is the direct playbook: reward crowdfunding (Kickstarter) for a specific new SKU or launch; once there's real recurring B2B/DTC revenue, either a "community round" (Wefunder/Crowdcube, turning customers into owners) or revenue-based finance (Wayflyer/Outfund) for inventory/working capital without dilution.

**Chef Burger** — benchmark against Honest Burgers (£2.9m raised Oct 2023 against a £1m target, 3,455 investors, funded regional expansion + a new format) when it's ready to scale via crowd equity.

**Cross-cutting warning for every raise**: the two most important cautionary tales are **BrewDog** (brilliant marketing, but the 2026 Tilray sale wiped out all 220,000 retail investors to £0 because a 2017 institutional round's liquidation preference sat above them — deal structure/preference stacking matters as much as the campaign) and **Emoov** (mid-campaign valuation cut under time pressure — investor communication integrity matters throughout, not just at launch).

## Quick-reference numbers (see reference docs for full detail and sourcing)

- **Pre-launch soft-commitment target**: 20-25% of goal (reward) / ~50% via a lead investor (equity) — the single biggest predictor of success.
- **SEIS**: 50% investor income tax relief, £200k/investor/yr cap, company must be ≤3yrs old, ≤£250k lifetime raise, ≤£350k gross assets, <25 employees.
- **EIS**: 30% relief, £1m/investor/yr (£2m if Knowledge Intensive), company can raise up to £10m/yr and £24m lifetime (KIC: £20m/yr, £40m lifetime) as of the 2026/27 tax year.
- **Crowdcube**: ~7-8.5% all-in success fee, no cost to list, UK's largest reach (2m+ investors). Crowdcube and Seedrs never merged — Seedrs is now "Republic Europe," a separate US-owned platform.
- **Folk2Folk**: £100k-£10m, 7.5-9% fixed, property-secured, explicitly names hospitality as a core sector.
- **Community pub raise**: typically £150k-£450k from 100-400 local investors, £50-£250 minimum tickets, 6 weeks-several months.
- **UK equity crowdfunding ads are regulated financial promotions** — FCA banned cash referral incentives (Feb 2023/Apr 2024); product/service perks (BrewDog's bar-discount model) remain legal. Reward-based crowdfunding has no such restriction.
