# Pillar 3: Offer + Copy — The Triple Tap

Hardest pillar. This is NOT normal copywriting: the wrong word doesn't just lose the sale, it lands you in spam. Your goal is NOT to sell — it's to identify if the lead has a problem you can solve and get them to **raise their hand**.

Overall vibe: sound like an email to a friend, not a sales pitch. Middle-school reading level. Get them to reply with one word, one hand, in under a minute.

## The Triple Tap — 3 actions the lead must take

Split every email into these 3 and test each chunk separately:

### Tap 1 — OPEN (happens in the preview text)
Only two things control the open: **subject line** + **first sentence** (preview text). Since we don't track opens, use **reply rate as the proxy** and A/B test.
- **Never signal the sale.** If they can tell you're selling before opening → trash/spam. The email should read like it could be from a client, vendor, or partner.
- **Questions get opens** (human instinct to answer). Old faithful: `question` + first name.
- Don't over-bait ("your house is on fire") then pivot to a pitch — instant spam.
- Examples (selling cold email): subject `Sending cold emails?` / first line `Not sure if you're sending these or not, {{firstName}}, mind checking?`
- Example (SEO): subject `Is this you on Google?` / first line `Just came across this, can you confirm it's you?`

### Tap 2 — READ (the body)
Seconds to catch attention + build trust before trash/spam. Accomplish TWO things in 2–3 sentences:
1. **Call out their problem + your exact mechanism** (how you solve it).
2. **Build trust/credibility authentically** (not braggadocious).

Personalize the **problem** (you understand their day-to-day) and the **social proof** (name-drop a competitor/name they'd recognize in their exact niche). No case studies yet? Use: social following, a YouTube video, media coverage, or do free work to get one anchor case study.

### Tap 3 — ACT (the CTA)
Low-resistance. One-word reply: "yes" / "sure" / "send it". No links, no clicking, no thinking in email #1. Example: `Would it be cool if I send over the strategy?`

## Golden rules

- ≤6 sentences. 6th-grade reading level (use Claude to simplify).
- Match **tonality** to the ICP profile (age, gender, industry — humor for a young agency owner, professional for a car dealer).
- **Do:** low-resistance CTA, real credibility, specific problem + mechanism.
- **Don't:** overpromise ("100 leads or money back" with no mechanism = instant spam), be vague, confuse, bait-and-switch, use promotional/salesy language.

## Personalization

- **Always use first name.** That's the tried-and-true one.
- **Be careful with company name + location** — "LLC/Corp/ALL CAPS" or a wrong city screams "cold email" and kills the campaign.
- **More personalization ≠ better** — each variable is another chance to be wrong or look automated.
- **AI personalization done wrong hurts more than it helps** (95% of people do it wrong). See `advanced.md`. Only 2–8 AI words, based on hard-to-find research, never the whole email.

## Spintax (mandatory)

Randomizes words/phrases so ESPs can't fingerprint repeat copy. Format: `{{random|hi|hello|hey}}`. 
- Generate via ChatGPT/Claude or the sending tool's spintax writer, then **preview all variations** — some combos read wrong.
- Run through a **spam-word checker** (tool's built-in, or Mailmeteor/EmailGuard) and replace flagged words.
- Never trust the tool's full AI-sequence-writer for the whole email — split-test against it at most.

## Sequences

- **3 emails max** (more emails = more spam risk; email #1 is almost always the best performer).
- **Shorter sequence** (1–2 emails) = reach more people; use with large TAM + cheap data.
- **Extended (4–7)** only with small TAM AND real new value/social proof + a soft opt-out in each email; pull different pain levers.
- Structure:
  - **Email 1:** full Triple Tap, text only. Test heavily.
  - **Email 2:** simple nudge, **same thread**, 1–3 days later ("did you catch my last email?").
  - **Email 3:** the info dump — now you can add links/video/case studies (you're likely already in the inbox). Same or new thread (test it). 3–5 days later.

## Links, images, opt-out (deliverability)

- **Email 1:** text only. No links, images, unsubscribe, or opt-out language.
- **Emails 2–3:** you're already in the inbox — links/images now OK if useful. Only high-trust links (YouTube, Calendly, Loom). Never a link on your brand-new cold domain. To send a video, ask "ok if I send a video?" as the CTA.
- **Once they reply, it's no longer cold** — send anything.
- **CAN-SPAM:** need a way to opt out + a mailing address + honor removals. Jay has never seen a real fine in 10+ years even for violations, but opt-out language in email #1 still hurts deliverability — so keep it out of #1, soft-language it in later emails.

## Split testing (the right way)

- **Campaigns** = test audiences (industry, company size — same copy, different lists).
- **A/B variants** = test copy/offer within a campaign (same list).
- Change **ONE variable at a time** (subject → body → CTA). To test 3× each of 3 elements = 9 emails.
- **First thing to test = whole different offers/lead magnets** (biggest swings), same subject line.
- **Judge on positive reply rate / opportunity rate**, NOT raw reply rate (a 10% reply that's all "f off" loses to a 5% reply that's all positive).
- Use ChatGPT to check **statistical significance** before picking a winner (screenshot the analytics → ask).
- Keep a running AI thread of winners/losers so it can suggest next tests.
