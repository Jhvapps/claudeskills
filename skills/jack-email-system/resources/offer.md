# The Offer

The best solution is a genuinely great offer (unique, solves a real problem, blue ocean) — then you don't need tricks. Most offers aren't there, so use the tools below.

## Understand the prospect first

They're not looking for you. Even a perfect-fit lead probably doesn't need it right now or uses a competitor. Goal of the cold email is NOT the sale — it's to get them to **raise their hand** and say "tell me more." Making the sale your email's goal = failure.

## Offer killers (instant spam / ignore)

- **Boring** — seen it a million times, no spark.
- **Competitive/commodity** — same as everyone, differentiated only on price.
- **Absurd** — insane guarantees with no credibility or mechanism ("six figures in 30 days or 150% back").
- **Complex** — if they can't understand it fast, they won't try.

## If your offer is boring + competitive: get your foot in the door

Break your service into bullet points → find the single most **desirable** piece nobody else emphasizes → lead with it as a **front-end offer**, then upsell the real (retainer) offer once you've delivered.

Three strategies:

1. **Loss leader** — sell something they want and think is expensive at a shockingly low price (at/near cost). e.g. a $97 done-for-you setup, or a Forbes backlink they think costs $6k for $1k. Make no money on it; win on back-end upsells + trust.

2. **Trojan horse** — get in the door under a non-sales pretense. People go defensive at a pitch. Frame it as a partnership, referral, or (Otter PR's top campaign) email as a **journalist** wanting to write their story rather than a PR agency selling them. Then transition to the sale. Ask: "how do I get in the door under a pretense that isn't 'buy my thing'?"

3. **Lead magnets** — see below.

## If you're new with no offer

Spend real time here — a better offer makes lead gen, sales, and delivery all easier.
- Sell something **unique** to people with money that others ignore.
- Reposition a generic skill: SEO → "rank you inside ChatGPT/Claude (LLM SEO)"; cold email skill → "influencer-outreach machines for ecom" instead of generic lead gen.

## Lead magnets

A resource / mini-service / free trial related to your offer that solves PART of the problem — good enough they'd pay for it.
- **Perceived value = time they think YOU spent** ÷ time THEY must spend consuming it. Handle this ratio to explain why a magnet flops.
- **When to use:** competitive/commodity offers, smaller businesses (enterprise won't consume a PDF), or when other frameworks failed.

Tiers:
- **Tier 3 (avoid):** generic templates, videos, case studies, white papers (unpersonalized, high consume-time). Exceptions: AI prompts, automation/n8n blueprints.
- **Tier 2 (maybe):** mini-courses, free trials, usable data (e.g. "8M free leads").
- **Tier 1 (do):** full course/community access, free software license, an actual sample of your service.

Present V1 magnets as: "want me to send over the [X]?" → they say yes → send.

## Reverse lead magnets (RLM) — the 2025 upgrade

The insight behind the old "Loom video" strategy: the prospect valued it because they thought you spent time making something **just for them**. RLMs deliver that custom-made feeling **at scale** via AI automation.

- Instead of "want the PDF?", say: **"I'd like to spend some time researching and putting this together for you — is that okay?"**
- They think you're investing effort → perceived value spikes → replies soar. This is the only thing that cracked 2–3% reply rates selling cold-email services (historically the hardest thing to sell by cold email).
- Requires AI-automation skill to actually generate + deliver the custom asset (see `advanced.md`). Warning: needs technical setup; not for the lazy/uncreative.
